The scene is playing out in a lot of companies right now. Eight months ago an AI notetaker was rolled out, and since then it sits in every sales call, dutiful, reliable, transcribing everything. By now there are 500 transcripts sitting in the tool. Then the question in the audit: what have you learned from them? Silence. A few reps have gone back and reread one of their own calls. That was it.

The thinking error starts at the purchase. Transcripts were never the product. Nobody reads 500 transcripts, just like nobody looks at 340 reports, and a mountain of unstructured text is not insight, it's raw material waiting to be processed. Whoever rolls out a notetaker without first defining the structural goal is buying expensive storage on a subscription model.

Structural goal means, concretely, three questions before the rollout. Which fields should get populated from every call? Budget mentioned or not, time horizon, competitors named, the two most frequent objections. Which signals matter beyond the individual call? Whether more than one person on the customer side speaks, for example, correlates hard with close probability in almost every B2B business. And where does all of it flow? Into CRM fields that show up in the forecast and in reporting, not into a separate tool with its own login that nobody opens after three weeks.

Once those three questions are answered, the notetaker becomes a pipeline: transcript in, structured fields out, written to the CRM via API, analyzable across all deals in a quarter. Technically that's surprisingly little work these days, an LLM call with a clean extraction schema plus field mapping, days rather than weeks. The hard part isn't the tech, it's the decision about which five fields really matter. No tool can make that one for you.

That leaves the privacy aftertaste, and it's no footnote. Recorded customer conversations are personal data, often collected without solid consent, stored indefinitely, frequently with a US vendor. A pile of data with no use isn't neutral, it's risk with no upside. The clean rule: process the transcript, keep the structured data, delete the raw material after 90 days. Then you have the insight without the archive.

500 conversations with real customers are the most valuable dataset your company owns. Right now, for most, it sits as a wall of text in a tab nobody opens. What exactly was the plan again?
