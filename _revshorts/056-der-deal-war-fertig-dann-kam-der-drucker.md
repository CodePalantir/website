The customer said yes verbally. The contract goes out, as a PDF attachment, with a request for signature. At the customer's office, it gets printed. The managing director who has to sign is traveling until Thursday. Thursday he signs, Friday the assistant scans it, but only the signature page, the other eleven are missing. Monday your AE follows up. Wednesday the complete scan arrives, fed in crooked, page nine twice. Twelve days between the yes and a signed contract. It is 2026.

Twelve days sound like a nuisance, but they are a risk. A verbal yes is a snapshot. In the time between, life happens: the competitor calls one more time, the CFO asks a question in the hallway, another project suddenly eats the budget. Every day between commitment and signature is a day the deal can come apart again. Closing momentum is real, and the customer's printer is its natural enemy.

The second cost is quieter. For those twelve days, the contract's status lives exclusively in the AE's inbox. The CRM says Negotiation, the truth says signed and sitting in the scanner folder. Forecast calls guess. Billing waits for a shout-out. And if the AE gets sick, nobody knows where the contract currently is, plain and simple.

eSignature solves both problems at once, but only if you build it all the way through. Everyone knows the half setup: DocuSign license purchased, document uploaded manually, link sent by email, and a human still checks the status. That is the old process with a new logo. End-to-end means: the contract is generated and sent from the opportunity, every status change writes back to the CRM automatically, both signatures trigger the next step, opportunity to Won, document archived, billing triggered. No shout-out, no manual filing, no chasing.

The integration between signature tool and CRM is standard craft, a few days of work, less depending on your stack. Afterward you can measure, for the first time, what was invisible before: time from send to signature, per segment, per contract type. At most companies that number drops from weeks to hours after the switch.

Go back through your last ten closes: how many days sat between the yes and the final signature, and what exactly was your forecast showing during that time?
