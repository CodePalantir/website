The whole story is right there in Amplitude. The account stopped using the dashboard feature eight weeks ago. Of twelve active users, four are left. The weekly core workflow that was the selling point at purchase last ran in April. Every one of those curves is cleanly tracked, versioned, queryable.

And the CSM? Before the customer call, he opens his CS tool, sees contract value, renewal date, and the notes from the last conversation. Then he calls and asks: "So, how are things going on your end?" He asks because he doesn't know. The customer answers politely and vaguely, both hang up, and the system afterwards says "all good, customer happy." Eight weeks later the cancellation arrives and everyone is surprised, except Amplitude.

Proactive customer success is the promise of every CS organization and every CS platform. But proactivity requires seeing things before the customer says them. Without product data in the CS view, that is simply impossible, and then proactivity isn't a standard, it's a fairy tale you tell each other in the team meeting. What happens instead is reactivity with a good calendar: you check in regularly, but blind.

Why the data doesn't flow rarely has anything to do with will. Amplitude and Mixpanel belong to the product team, bought for funnel analyses and feature decisions. The CRM belongs to sales, the CS platform to customer success. Three systems, three owners, three budgets, and the question of who builds the stretch in between has no natural addressee. So nobody builds it.

It doesn't take total integration. Nobody wants 400 events per user in the CRM. A handful of condensed metrics per account is enough: active users relative to licenses, core feature usage in trend, days since last relevant activity. Aggregates like that can be written straight to the account via reverse ETL or a slim pipeline, right where the CSM already works. A few days of engineering, and afterwards every customer conversation has a basis in fact. The call then no longer starts with "how are things going" but with "I noticed that team X on your side hasn't been in the product since May, what happened there".

The difference between those two conversation openers is the difference between care and attendance. Which of the two are you paying for right now?
