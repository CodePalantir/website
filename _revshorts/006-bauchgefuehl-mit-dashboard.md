The Monday morning forecast call. Every AE walks through their deals, says commit or best case, the sales lead nods, adjusts down 15 percent by gut feeling, and the number goes to leadership. At the end of the quarter it's off by a third. As always.

The reflexive diagnosis is: we need a forecasting tool. Clari, BoostUp, something with AI. And the reflexive diagnosis is wrong.

Look at the data underneath your forecast before you talk about tools. There are opportunities sitting in the commit category whose close date has been March 31 for two quarters, even though it's July. Half of the open deals are missing a next step, which translated means: nobody knows what happens next, but the deal is in the forecast at 60 percent anyway. And those 60 percent, where do they actually come from? From the stage definition someone once set up with default values. Your weighted pipeline multiplies real amounts by fantasy probabilities. The result has two decimal places and zero meaning.

A forecast is not a number a tool spits out. A forecast is a chain of data fields somebody maintains, sometimes honestly, sometimes not. Close date, stage, amount, next step, last customer signal. If those fields contain garbage, every tool in the world aggregates garbage. Buying Clari doesn't fix that. Clari just turns empty fields into a pretty chart faster. Gut feeling with a dashboard, now for an extra 60,000 euros a year.

Forecast quality is data model quality. That's the whole thesis, and it's uncomfortable because it means work instead of shopping. Stages tied to observable customer actions instead of AE optimism. A close date that gets automatically challenged when it's pushed for the third time. Validations that simply won't allow a commit without a next step. Probabilities from your own win rate history instead of factory settings. All buildable, in Salesforce and in HubSpot alike, natively, without a single new license.

Above 300 sellers the math changes, that's where Clari plays to its strengths. You don't have 300 sellers.

Run the test: take your current commit and check just two fields on every deal, close date and next step. Count how many have both properly maintained. That ratio is your real forecast. The rest is decoration.
