The deal was in the forecast for March. Now it says June, and in the meeting someone asks: since when, exactly? Silence. The AE thinks it's been that way for a while. The sales director could have sworn it still said Q1 last week. Nobody can look it up, because the close date is a field like any other: it shows the current value and says nothing about everything before it.

That silence costs you more than you think.

Without field history on the critical fields, you only ever see the last frame of a movie. When was the amount halved from 80k to 45k? How many times has this deal already wandered through the stages, back and forth? Did the rep push the close date on the last day of the month to save the forecast, or three weeks earlier because the customer communicated honestly? The difference is first-class coaching material, and it's written down nowhere.

Yet the fix costs almost nothing. Salesforce, HubSpot, Dynamics: they can all track field history, you just have to switch it on, and selectively at that. Not on 200 fields, that's noise. On the five that count: close date, amount, stage, forecast category, owner. Fully configured in an hour, coffee included.

What you get for it is a flight recorder. After two quarters, patterns lie in the open that used to be gut feeling. One rep moves close dates 2.3 times per deal on average, always just before quarter end. Another never touches them, and her forecast lands within 5 percent. Deals that fall back a stage twice close at only 8 percent. Numbers like that turn forecast calls from an exchange of opinions into analysis.

A side effect that will soon become the main effect: every AI forecasting model you want to introduce in the coming years learns from exactly this history. If you don't record today, tomorrow you'll be training a model with no past. The data from 2026 can't be bought back in 2028, not for any money in the world.

And yes, the whole thing has an uncomfortable side. History makes visible who pushes and who delivers. That's why the resistance to it is rarely technical. A field that remembers what was is incorruptible, and not everyone on the team likes that.

If someone asks tomorrow when that one deal slipped from Q1 to Q3: can you answer, or do you shrug?
