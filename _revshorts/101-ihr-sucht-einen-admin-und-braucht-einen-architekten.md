The job posting always reads the same. "Salesforce Administrator, 45,000 euros." Below that, the wish list: evolve the data model, maintain integrations to billing and marketing, rethink processes across departments, introduce CPQ down the line. That's not an admin role. That's an architect role at an admin salary, and the market answers it accordingly.

Who applies to a posting like that? Someone who can create fields, manage users, click reports together, and bring a Trailhead profile with a decent stack of badges. All honorable skills. Many people can click. Few people can think in systems, and the few cost double, because they know what they're worth.

The difference doesn't show on day one, it shows after 18 months. In that time the admin has implemented every request, diligently and fast. That's exactly the problem. Every request implemented individually means: 80 new fields, 30 of which store the same thing in slightly different shades, four automations triggering each other, a permissions concept made of special cases. No single step was wrong. The sum is a system nobody can explain anymore, least of all the person who built it.

An architect would have said no in a dozen places. Not out of laziness, but because he sees the question behind the request: you don't want a new field, you want to know why deals get stuck in stage three, and there are already three fields for that, two of which can go. That no is the most expensive skill in all of ops, and it appears in no job posting.

Now the uncomfortable part: the obvious solution, just hire an architect, is usually wrong for a company of 80. You don't have 40 hours of architecture work per week. You have maybe 40 hours per quarter, but every week you have user questions, reports, small adjustments. A full-time architect gets bored at your company and leaves after a year, the 45k admin drowns and builds debt.

What actually fits: admin capacity for the day-to-day, in-house or part-time, plus architecture as a targeted, expensive, rare ingredient from outside. A few days per quarter in which someone with hands-on scars straightens out the data model and sets the guardrails within which the admin can then click safely.

Your system today looks the way your job posting was worded two years ago. Read it again. Does it say think or click?
