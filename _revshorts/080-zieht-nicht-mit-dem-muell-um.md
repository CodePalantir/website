In every CRM migration, the same sentence eventually comes up: "Let's take everything with us for now, we can sort it out later." Sounds cautious. It's the most expensive decision of the entire project.

Because taking everything means: moving the trash and paying for the move. Every record you migrate wants to be mapped, transformed, validated, and tested. The lead from 2019 who never replied and whose company has since gone under costs exactly as much mapping effort as your best customer. You're paying engineering hours to carry dead data into a fresh system, where it then keeps doing what dead data does: create duplicates, distort reports, eat storage limits, and feed scoring models with noise.

A real-world example, numbers lightly rounded. 180,000 leads in the legacy system. Touched in the last 24 months: 31,000. The rest was trade-show lists from 2018, purchased records from a long-abandoned outbound experiment, and triple duplicates of the same 40 corporations. Migrating all 180,000 would have nearly tripled the mapping project. In the end, the 31,000 got migrated and the rest went into an archive as a CSV export. Cold, searchable, retained GDPR-compliant or deleted, depending on the legal situation. Nobody missed them. Not one person.

And that's the real point: the migration is the best opportunity to throw things away you will ever get. In day-to-day operations, nobody cleans up, because cleanup has no sponsor and sales is afraid there might still be something in there. During a migration, every object is on the table anyway. There's a budget, there's a cutoff date, there's someone who has to touch every field. This opportunity won't come around again for the next seven years.

An archive is not a dumpster, by the way, that's part of being honest here. Old customer data with contract history needs an orderly place, just not as active records in the new CRM, where they surface in every search, every filter, and every campaign audience. A warehouse export or a simple read-only archive is almost always enough. Whoever genuinely needs the record in three years will find it there in two minutes.

So the question before the migration is not "what do we take with us" but "what has the new system earned." If you had to set up your CRM from scratch tomorrow: how much of what's in it today would you voluntarily enter again?
