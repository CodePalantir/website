The pitches all sound the same. An AI SDR that prospects around the clock, personalizes, follows up, never calls in sick, and costs a tenth. Sounds like the end of the sales problem. And it is, for the three percent of companies that didn't have one to begin with.

Because the thing doesn't learn sales. It learns you.

An AI SDR is an amplifier, not a salesperson. It takes your messaging, your target account logic, your sequences, and multiplies them by a large factor. If your best email today pulls an eight percent reply rate because it hits a real problem and sounds like a human, the machine now writes three hundred of those a day instead of twenty. Great. But if your emails were generic to begin with, you know the ones, the "I came across your profile" ones, you're now producing generic junk at industrial speed. Spam in series, with your domain as the sender.

And the domain remembers. Deliverability holds grudges.

The real test comes before the tool purchase and has nothing to do with AI. Write down your playbook. Who exactly is the target customer, not as an industry but as a situation: what just happened to them that would make them listen right now? Which problem does the first email address, in what tone, with what proof? What happens after a reply, what after silence? If that document doesn't exist, or consists of four bullet points from the last offsite, the AI SDR has nothing to learn from. It hallucinates a playbook instead, and that sounds exactly like the emails you delete every day yourself.

We see it in audits regularly. Company buys an AI SDR, spins up 40 inboxes, three weeks later two domains are on blacklists and the reply rate sits at 0.4 percent. The model wasn't at fault. It dutifully scaled what was there. There just wasn't anything there.

The reverse holds too, and that's the good news: a team with a sharp ICP, clean data, and messaging that demonstrably gets replies can genuinely triple output with AI sequencing without hiring three people. The order decides. First prove the playbook, manually, with twenty emails a week and real replies. Then plug in the machine.

Whoever flips the order isn't buying an SDR. They're buying a photocopier and feeding it a blank page.
