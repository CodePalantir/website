Sometime in the last two years, many companies renamed their Sales Ops role. New title on LinkedIn, new signature, same ticket queue. The colleague who used to create fields in the CRM and build reports for the head of sales now, as Revenue Operations Manager, creates fields in the CRM and builds reports for the head of sales. That's not RevOps. That's a rebranding.

The difference isn't in the title, it's in the scope. Sales Ops ends where sales ends. RevOps starts at the first marketing touch and doesn't end until the renewal, and everything in between belongs to it: the MQL definition marketing and sales have been fighting over for years, the handoff where leads leak away every week, the question of why churn data from the CS tool never lands in the CRM, which is why sales cheerfully calls existing customers who canceled last week. One revenue process, one data model, one owner.

The test takes a minute. Ask who at your company authoritatively defines what a qualified lead is. If the answer is that marketing counts for itself and sales counts for itself and the quarterly meeting is spent arguing over the difference, you don't have RevOps, no matter what the org chart says. Second question, sharper still: does the person carrying RevOps in their title even have access to the marketing automation and the CS platform? Often not even read access. The scope ends at the system boundary, and the system boundary runs exactly where it ran before the renaming.

The rebranding isn't harmless either, it does damage. With the title the expectations rise, the mandate stays the same, and after a year the verdict is that RevOps didn't work here. Except it was never tried. All that was tried was whether the same ticket queue gets worked down faster under a more modern name. It doesn't. Why would it.

If you're serious, you give the role three things: access to every system along the lifecycle, authority over the funnel definitions, and veto power on new tools and processes, including against the head of sales. That costs surrendered power in three departments. Which is exactly why the renaming is so much more popular than the implementation.

Whether you have RevOps isn't written in the org chart. It shows in who gets called when marketing and customer success are fighting over a number. Can't think of anyone?
