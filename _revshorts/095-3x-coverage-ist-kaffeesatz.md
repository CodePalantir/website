Every sales leader knows the rule: pipeline should cover three times the quota, then the quarter will work itself out. 4.2 million in pipeline against a 1.4 million target, looks good, box checked. Except the 4.2 million isn't pipeline. It's a graveyard with price tags.

Run the exercise honestly, once. Pull out every deal whose close date has already slipped three times. Then the ones without a single logged activity in the last 60 days. Then the ones sitting in stage 3 since February, because the rep doesn't want to close them, that feels like giving up, but can't move them either. In an audit last year, of 4.2 million exactly 1.9 remained. Real coverage: 1.35. The quarter was mathematically lost long ago, only nobody knew, because the dashboard showed green.

The insidious part: everyone involved has a reason to let the zombies live. The rep keeps the deal because a full pipeline looks like diligence and shortens the forecast conversation. The team lead doesn't push a cleanup as long as the coverage number decorates his own slide. And leadership looks at the multiplier instead of the deals behind it, because the multiplier was invented for exactly that: not having to look at the deals behind it anymore.

That's why pipeline hygiene doesn't work as an appeal. It works as a system rule. A field that counts how often a close date has been pushed and flags the deal from the third time on. A flow that puts deals without activity on a review list after 45 days. Stage definitions with exit criteria a rep can't argue away. That's a week of configuration work in the CRM, not a culture initiative, and it changes behavior faster than any sales meeting where data hygiene gets requested for the umpteenth time.

Only once that's running is the coverage calculation worth doing at all. And then you're also allowed to doubt the multiplier itself. 3x is a rule of thumb from a world with a 33 percent win rate. If you actually win 20 percent, you need more like 5x. If you win 40, you cruise comfortably at 2.5x. The right number is in your own closing history, not in some sales influencer's LinkedIn post.

An inflated pipeline with 3x coverage reliably soothes until week eleven of the quarter. Then the cushion turns into a hole.
