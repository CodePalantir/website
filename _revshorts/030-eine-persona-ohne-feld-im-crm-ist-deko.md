Somewhere in your SharePoint sits a deck from a persona workshop. Slide 14: "Marketing Mary, 38, Head of Marketing, likes efficiency, listens to podcasts, hates cold calling." Next to it, a stock photo. Two days of workshop, an agency, a decent budget. And now the test question: open your CRM and filter for all contacts who are Marketing Mary.

Can't do it? Then Mary doesn't exist. Not operationally, anyway. She lives in a slide deck, and slide decks don't segment campaigns, don't route leads, and don't show up in any report. A persona you can't filter by is wall decoration with a budget history.

The mistake is not in the concept. Personas are perfectly fine as a thinking tool, the only question is where they end up. Almost always they end with the marketing team as shared vocabulary for content planning, and there they evaporate. Nothing makes it into the system. No field, no mapping, no logic. The campaign then goes out to everyone again, because "everyone" is the only segment you can click.

Operationalizing is unspectacular, and that is exactly why it gets skipped. A picklist field on the contact, three to five values, and the structure is done. It doesn't get filled by hand, that doesn't last two months, but through an automation that maps job titles to personas: everything with "Marketing" or "Demand" becomes Mary, everything with "Managing Director" or "CEO" becomes the Owner, the rest lands in a review list. Title mappings like these are ugly, grateful grunt work, a rule list of a hundred lines that you build once and sharpen quarterly.

And then something interesting happens: the persona starts working. You can measure conversion per persona and might discover that Mary downloads the most whitepapers but the managing director signs the deals. You can split nurture tracks, build routing rules, cut win/loss reviews by persona. All things that are impossible on slide 14.

A side effect of the exercise: whatever can't be translated into a field gets thrown out. Podcast preferences and favorite colors don't survive operationalization, and that's a good thing, because they never influenced a decision. What remains are two or three roles in the buying process with clear identifying markers. Less poetry, more filters.

So: how many personas did your last workshop produce, and how many of them can your CRM find today? The difference is your decoration ratio.
